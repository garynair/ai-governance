# Contributing

Thank you for considering a contribution. This list spans two things: AI governance for regulated environments, and runtime governance for AI agents. An entry needs to materially help a practitioner in one of those.

## What belongs here

- Regulations, frameworks, and standards practitioners must comply with.
- Standards bodies and working groups where the technical conversation in this space happens.
- Research papers that have become reference points in industry practice (not just academic citations).
- Open-source tools and primitives a regulated engineering team, or a team running production AI agents, would actually use.
- Commercial governance platforms with a mature regulator-facing posture, or enterprise platforms for AI risk, governance, and compliance.
- AI security and red-team tools, observability tools, audit tools, and cost-control tools.
- Architecture patterns for safe production agent deployment.
- Regulators, policy-making bodies, and industry programs.
- Conferences, newsletters, and books that practitioners actually read.

## What does not belong here

- Pure AI ethics commentary, opinion essays, alignment philosophy, or manifestos without tooling. Other lists curate those.
- Generic AI news sites or aggregators.
- CI/CD gates over AI-generated code (pre-merge, not runtime).
- Courses or tutorials on building agents without a governance focus.
- Tools or platforms that are early-stage and not yet usable in production.
- Vendor marketing content without a substantive free tier or open-source component, or self-promotion of products you have not used or do not work with.
- Affiliate links of any kind.
- Anything paywalled where equivalent content is freely available elsewhere.

## How to submit

1. Open an issue titled `[Submission]: Resource Name`, or open a PR directly.
2. Add the entry in alphabetical order within the relevant section, in the format: `- [Name](URL) - one sentence on what it is, one sentence on why a practitioner would care.`
3. No marketing language, no unverified performance claims.
4. If you are the author or maintainer of the resource, disclose that in the issue or PR description. It's fine to add your own; we just like to know.

## Style

- American English in the description copy.
- No em-dashes used as sentence punctuation.
- No emojis.
- Avoid banned vocabulary (delve, leverage, harness, robust, seamless, holistic, transformative, paradigm). Plain language wins.

## Quality bar

- Must be publicly accessible (open-source or free tier), or a mature regulator-facing commercial platform.
- Must be actively maintained (commit within 12 months, or a standards body with an active update cycle).
- Description must be factual.

## Review

A maintainer reviews PRs within seven days. Most PRs that meet the criteria above land within two weeks. PRs that need clarification stay open for up to thirty days; after that they close with a polite note.
